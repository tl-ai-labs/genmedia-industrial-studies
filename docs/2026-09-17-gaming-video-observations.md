# What the gaming videos look like: Gemini Omni Flash vs Seedance 2.5

Run `2026-09-17_121726_video`, 25 scenarios (BGMI, Real Cricket 24, GTA V), one clip
per scenario per model, rated blind by Gemini 3 Flash. These observations come from
the judge's written reasoning on every clip and from viewing frames of the inputs and a
sample of outputs; nobody has yet watched all 49 clips end to end. Same format as
`video/runs/merged-video-all/OBSERVATIONS-simple.md`.

## Plain-language observations

1. **Both models look like the game, most of the time.** Lighting, kit, stadium and
   street detail match the input still or clip closely; reference fidelity is the
   highest-scoring criterion for both.
2. **Gemini cuts, Seedance holds the shot.** On five image-to-video scenarios the judge
   docked Gemini for inserting cuts or a montage (boundary catch, batsman six, night
   chase, mass jump, rooftop), which changes faces and kit between shots. Seedance kept
   one continuous take in every scenario it completed.
3. **Gemini is the safer editor.** It won 7 of the 11 edits both completed. Seedance
   applied no slow-motion at all, turned the smoothing edit into ghosting, left the
   buggy's dust trail behind, and rebuilt the whole street when asked only to relight
   it for night.
4. **Gemini's edits sometimes rebuild the scene instead of editing it.** The kill-feed
   blur came back as a new clip rather than the source footage, the sunset edit
   changed the car's body and badges, and the pedestrian removal did nothing at all.
5. **Seedance is the safer animator of a still.** It won 7 of the 12 image-to-video
   scenarios both completed and never scored below 79%; Gemini's worst still-to-video
   result was 58% (the boundary catch).
6. **Running and jumping are the weakest motion for both.** Characters slide along the
   rooftop instead of running, robbers teleport into the car, and a second plane
   appeared out of nowhere in Gemini's mass jump.
7. **Hands and fingers warp on both.** Clapping fans, the rifle reload and the dugout
   celebration all show fingers sliding, blurring or passing through each other.
8. **Small on-screen text drifts.** Jersey names and numbers garbled on both models
   during fast movement; Seedance's score bar started from the wrong total and wiped
   in rather than sliding in.
9. **Broadcast-style graphics are fine when the instruction is simple.** Both added the
   speed readout correctly; Gemini timed it exactly to the release, Seedance half a
   second late.
10. **Seedance refuses photoreal groups of faces.** It declined the dugout still as
    private information; Gemini accepted it. Neither model refused any prompt.

## Internal only

- **Speed and cost.** Gemini p50 86 s per clip against 230 s for Seedance; $1.10 against
  $3.44 per clip. Seedance edits billed about $2.71 each, far below the $10.50 cap the
  pre-flight assumed.
- **Brand text in a generated input.** The judge notes an "Opel" badge and "Insignia"
  lettering on the car in the coastal-drive source clip (VID-GEDIT-03), generated by
  Veo 3.1. The brand check covers prompts, not generated files; add generated inputs to
  the check (gap log 18).
- **Sample size.** One generation per scenario and 24 scenarios compared. Per-scenario
  results are indicative, not conclusive.
- **Judge timeouts on large clips.** Seedance's "remove the pedestrian" clip (20 MB) timed
  out three times at the runner's 300 s judge limit; a fourth attempt at 900 s rated it 82.5%.
  Raise the limit, or downscale clips for the judge (gap log 21).
- **Edit rubric only partly scored.** The judge returned no preservation score on any of
  the 24 judged edits and technical compliance is unmeasured on edits, so the runner scored
  edits on three criteria with redistributed weights (gap log 20).
